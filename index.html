Kafila قافلة · a screening-to-care accountability platform · Future Health Challenge 05

An abnormal result. A clear path to care.

A patient's stool test is positive. With Kafila:

  • The patient understands. Their clinic sends a secure message the same day and explains the result in their language. They can ask questions at any time.
  • The appointment fits the patient. For example, a female doctor, a weekend slot, covered by their plan.
  • No one is forgotten. If the patient does not reply: an SMS, then a call, then the coordinator phones.
  • Someone is responsible. A coordinator follows the case, and each step has a named person, until a doctor closes it with the report.

Sahatna, Abu Dhabi's health app, helps patients reach care. Kafila makes sure the next step actually happens: every case has a coordinator, a deadline from the national guideline and a verified outcome.

01Five steps

More than a reminder. One case, followed through to an outcome.

Kafila turns each result into one case that moves through five steps: record, prioritise, refer, navigate, track. Each step has a person responsible and a deadline, and a clock shows when it is late.

Every actionable result gets a next step, a responsible team and a documented outcome.

The five steps match the journey the Abu Dhabi Public Health Centre set out for Challenge 05 (challenge webinar, 29 Oct 2026, paraphrased).

02The gap

Too many patients are lost between screening and follow-up.

Screening finds the problem. Then the result can sit in a system where no one is clearly responsible for the next step. The person may be scared, unsure what it means, or unable to find a time that works, and a missed follow-up can go unnoticed.

The gap · Abu DhabiOnly 30.5%

completed a follow-up colonoscopy after a positive stool test.

A study of Emirati adults at SEHA primary care clinics in Abu Dhabi (2015–2016) found a major gap between screening and follow-up care: 436 people had the colonoscopy, 933 did not. Kafila measures today's gap and helps close it.

03Where it runs

On the patient's phone. On the clinic's computers. Designed to connect with Malaffi, Sahatna and the clinic's own systems.

No new device and no new download. The patient opens a secure link from a message; the care team uses any browser. In a pilot, results arrive from the clinic's systems. Once the clinic record is connected, the outcome is also written back to the patient's health record. The patient side can also live inside Sahatna, the Abu Dhabi health app, as the organisers suggested.

05Fatima's case

Meet Fatima, 52. Day 0.

Her stool test (FIT) is positive. Deadlines follow the UAE national guideline: referral within 15 working days, colonoscopy within 31 working days after the referral. The pilot agrees the final values with the clinical partner. These are real screens from the prototype, with synthetic data.

Day 0 · 09:00

A message from her own clinic

In the language her clinic has on record for her (in the pilot; the demo shows it in English). No result in the preview, only a secure link. If the number is wrong, "This is not me" stops all messages.

Privacy: nothing medical is shown before sign-in. In the pilot she signs in with UAE PASS or a one-time code, and every view and change goes to an audit log that cannot be edited.

Day 0 · 09:01

She agrees, or asks for a call

Consent is a free choice. She can say "I'd rather get a phone call" and Noura calls her instead.

Consent: always with an alternative.

Day 0 · 09:02

Her result, in plain words

"See a doctor." A small amount of hidden blood was found. It is not a diagnosis. Below: what happens next and by when.

Rules, not AI: the zone and the deadline come from the screening programme's rules table.

Day 0 · 09:12

"Is it cancer?"

The AI chat answers from prepared texts (pending clinical review): most people with a positive test do not have cancer, and a colonoscopy can find the cause. Every answer names what it is compared with and its source.

Understanding: a one-tap check that she knows the next step.

Day 0 · 09:13

What makes it hard to go?

Fear, cost, time off, the preparation, transport, privacy, or "I prefer a female doctor". She taps one, and the booking filters follow.

Barriers: the reasons people do not go, asked in the first minute.

Day 0 · 09:14

A slot that fits

Gastroenterology slots in her network, female doctors first, with what her plan covers. If nothing fits, one tap asks Noura to call and book for her.

Where the slots come from: in the pilot, the free times are read from the booking system of the gastroenterology clinics in her network, and what her plan covers from the insurer. In the demo both are simulated.

Day 0 → closure

The same steps her care team sees, in plain words, each with who is doing it now. After the colonoscopy, Dr. Hessa reviews the report with her and closes the case.

06Urgent cases

When it is urgent, Kafila does not wait for AI.

Rajesh, 45, works shifts. His blood pressure at screening is 185/115. Normal is below 120/80.

09:00

Rules mark it urgent. He sees "Needs care today: a doctor will call you before 13:00", in the call window he chose.

09:04

He types "chest pain". A fixed screen says call 998 now. The phrase list is checked before any AI, in Arabic and English. His care team is alerted.

+30 min

No answer to the doctor's call? Retry, then an SMS with the clinic's number.

Same day

Still nobody reached after 3 tries: the clinical lead gets the case today.

AI inside Kafila

AI that explains and drafts, inside rules that clinicians set.

  • In the demo: answers the patient's questions in Arabic or English, by text or voice, from prepared texts (pending clinical review).
  • In the pilot: rewrites the case summary and the referral letter in plain words for the doctor. Today these drafts are built from the case fields.
  • In the pilot: sorts what the patient writes into barriers (fear, cost, time…) and suggests the next action from a fixed list. A person clicks to accept.

If the AI is off or fails, the prepared answers take over and the loop keeps working.

Who gains, and how

People

Found earlier, less fear

  • A positive test is no longer lost between screening and follow-up.
  • Cancer found earlier: 5-year survival 91% when found localised vs 13% once it has spread (colon, ACS/SEER 2014–2020).
  • Answers in their language at any hour, an appointment that works for them, a female doctor if they prefer, and what their plan covers before they book.
Health system

Screening that pays off

  • Tests already paid for lead to care, instead of stopping at a result.
  • The programme lead sees who is lost and why: by language, district, insurance.
  • More people reach the procedure without hiring a navigator for every case. Navigation added 13 percentage points in PRECISE (55.1% vs 42.1%).
  • Safe by design: rules set urgency and deadlines, clinicians decide, AI explains, every step is logged.
Economy

Cheaper care, fuller clinics

  • Early-stage treatment is shorter and cheaper than late-stage treatment. The UAE figure is measured in the pilot.
  • Fewer empty endoscopy slots and no-shows.
  • Less coordinator time per case (target −50%, an assumption to test).
  • People stay at work: shorter treatment, less sick leave.
  • A base platform fee, plus a bonus for each follow-up completed and checked independently, adjusted for how complex the cases are. No reward for easy patients or paper closures.

Value to a payer per year = positive results × extra people who reach follow-up × share with disease found × saving from earlier stage + coordinator hours saved + no-shows avoided. Filled with the partner's own data in the pilot.

About this prototype

Built during the Future Health Digital Innovation Challenge by a solo founder with medical education, working with a team of AI roles: developer, tester, business analyst, architect, designer, medical reviewer, writer.

Concept and demonstration prototype. All people, results, clinics and insurance plans are synthetic. Clinical logic follows published guidelines and is pending review by a licensed clinician. Messages, booking and integrations are simulated. Not for clinical use and not registered as a medical device. Not affiliated with or endorsed by DoH Abu Dhabi or ADPHC.
